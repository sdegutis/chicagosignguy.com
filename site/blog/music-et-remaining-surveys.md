Blick doubled their poster board prices,
and started printing logos all over the back,
effectively quadrupling their price.

I also gave out my phone number in a previous article
as a social experiment, just to see what would happen.
Only one person texted me, a paradoxical young woman,
who now has everything she needs to inevitably cancel me.

So the writing's on the wall,
my surveys are nearing an end,
and I must finish my book soon.

For the sake of the book,
there are a few more topics
that I need to cover,
along with songs I think befit:

But I also have a very particular theory about music.
That the music that you love at any given point in your life
is an indication of where your heart is at, what you love,
or what you are struggling to understand or cope with.

For instance, back when I was in a one-sided relationship,
two songs I remember being in love with at the time,
were [Better Man] by Pearl Jam, and [Simple Kind of Life], by No Doubt.
I was not conscious at the time that she was merely settling for me,
nor of the fact that these two songs were shouting this fact to me.
Only in retrospect do I see an absolutely clear connection.

There are a few songs I still find extremely attractive,
which continue to draw me back to them perpetually.
My love for them is telling of something about me,
about the current state of my heart, my hopes or my fears.

Given that the entire point of my book is
to understand what it means to have healthy relationships,
to truly figure out what it means to love and be loved,
I see a connection between the songs I love and the questions I have.

Thus, several times this year, a survey topic for my book
has been directly inspired from a song
that I have been obsessing over for the prior few days.

There are still more songs that I need to understand,
and some that I just feel absolutely compelled to share,
often simultaneously for their meaning and their beauty.

There are also several songs that have in common
a sort of hauntingly beautiful, angelic chorus.
Even though they all reside in disparate genres,
their refrains feel painfully soft and feminine.

* [Return of the Phantom Stranger] -- Rob Zombie
* [Blood, Milk, and Sky] -- White Zombie
* [Subwoofer Lullaby] -- C418
* [Warmth] -- C418
* [Sol and Luna] -- Joep Beving
* [Shepherd] -- Joep Beving
* [Double Sky (Reprise)] -- Meredi
* [Somewhere Only We Know] -- rhianne

(I remember playing this version of
*Somewhere Only We Know*
to sing my daughters to sleep.)

It's entirely possible that,
by revealing this list of songs,
I'm giving away secrets of my heart,
perhaps too many.
But it's a risk I'm willing to take,
in order to share beauty that moves me.
