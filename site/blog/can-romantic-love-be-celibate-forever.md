About 2/3 of people say Yes,
and only 1/3 say No.

There are two sides,
worded slightly differently,
because the first side,
where it says "perpetually,"
seemed confusing to people,
who were saying Yes too easily.
So I worded it simpler on the back.

Any time people asked to clarify,
I said "*nothing* sexual, forever."
That changed a few minds:
some who almost put Yes put No.

## Discussions

I didn't ask anyone who put No,
or anyone clearly in a hurry,
for their in-depth answers.
Some people volunteered theirs.

Someone made the point that
if you're too old for sex,
you should still have the right
to fall in love and marry.

Someone made the point that
in places where it's illegal
or at least socially frowned upon
to be in a homosexual relationship,
such people have been celibate
in secret relationships with each other.

Someone made the point that
if you have sexual trauma,
you might not want sex,
but might still be in love.

Someone made the point that
you may be an asexual person,
that is, having no sexual desire,
yet still feel romantic love.

Someone made the point that
if your partner is disabled,
or becomes disabled while together,
you can still be in love with them.

One black man of about 60 who put No,
said in a very vulgar way that
he essentially can't control himself,
and that if he ends up sexually aroused,
he can't resist making love to his woman.

One person who put No asked, then
what's the point of a relationship?

One young woman who put Yes said,
"it's not for me, not my lifestyle,
but it can be for someone else."

One young man, who clearly goes to the gym,
who seemed to be eastern European of some kind,
probably an Orthodox Christian by his looks,
put Yes, saying we are more than animals,
and that love is higher than sexual pleasure.
He added that he doesn't drink anymore either,
and that it took him a year to get his creativity back
after he left his formerly hedonistic lifestyle.

A couple people misunderstood the question,
and said Yes, but that it's just *platonic* love.

A few people who walked by
answered verbally saying
"*romantic* love."
I have no idea what they meant,
and I think they misunderstood the survey.

One young woman who takes my survey often,
who seems to work somewhere nearby my spot,
put No, saying sex deepens the connection.
She said at some point in a relationship,
she needs to "experience" the whole person.

One young woman at the McDonald's in my hometown
looked like she would have a thoughtful answer,
because her aesthetic seems rather intentional,
so I explained my book and asked for her answer.
She said "I think it would be deeper."

## Inspiration

A few people asked what inspired the survey.
Depending on the person,
I gave different answers,
all of them being true of course.

I almost didn't do this survey,
because I thought I would be the only Yes.
And my Yes is entirely different from all the others,
it's one that nobody who took it has mentioned at all.

As a Catholic, I am required to believe
that Joseph and Mary, the parents of Jesus,
were voluntarily celibate for their whole lives,
both before and after marriage,
both having made vows of celibacy before marriage.

And I also choose to believe that
they had the strongest romance,
and the deepest love,
humanly possible.

And I choose to believe that
this type of romantic love
is still possible today.

In the middle ages, many couples imitated this,
and chose to take vows of celibacy after marriage.

So, in my ongoing search for a soulmate,
I figured that I might find her this way.
I thought everyone would put No,
except me and perhaps one young woman,
who would have the same reason.

I didn't anticipate all the other reasons
people had for maintaining a relationship
in the face of celibacy, voluntary or not.

And, of course, I didn't find my soulmate.

## Song of the Day

It was a bit of a cop-out song.
I couldn't find any song fitting
for the topic of voluntary celibacy,
although The Celibate Life by The Shins
has the word in the title, but that's it.

So I wanted to pick a relatively obscure song
that represented disillusionment with sex.
I looked through my recent songs and found three:

* [Hallelujah](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y8AWFf7EAc4) by Jeff Buckley
* [Take Me To Church](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1nnRC6jDOCI) by Hozier
* [Sweet Dreams](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QUvVdTlA23w) by Marilyn Manson

The first two seemed too mainstream,
and I like to share music people might not know.
But I also didn't look that hard,
and I felt bad sharing a musician with bad morals.

Still though, I can't think of another song for this.

I almost picked [Desires are Almost Memories](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Xg57-uDJ6q8)
by A Winged Victory For The Sullen,
which was a favorite of mine for like a month straight.
I love it for its subtle intensity,
which I think is a product of voluntary celibacy.

Now that I think about it,
I should have picked that one.
