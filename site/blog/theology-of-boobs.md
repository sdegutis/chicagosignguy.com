This past week, I told a few people
that there's a chapter in my upcoming book
about the "theology of boobs."
I worded it this way as a joke,
but I'm concerned I was misunderstood.

The famous Pope John Paul II had a series of lectures
which were called [Theology of the Body],
and which was later compiled into a [book].
He may be my favorite philosopher of all time,
and this is one of my favorite theology books ever.
He's also the author of [my favorite movie ever],
which is essentially a philosophical treatise,
and which I wrote an [article] and did a [survey] about.
I have chopped the movie up into clips
and uploaded them to my [TikTok] and [YouTube]
so you can watch it conveniently.

In this book, St. John Paul II
connects countless Bible verses together
to draw many conclusions
about personhood, gender,
sexuality, and marriage.
There is a chapter in my book about gender,
which draws on this a little further,
making new or passed over connections.

But it is both unfitting and unnecessary
for a Catholic theologian to be vulgar.
My book does not contain any such vulgarity.
So it causes me a little embarrassment
that I made that vulgar comment *twice* now.

As an example of a point my book makes,
it is interesting that we call the eye
a window into a person's soul,
just as Jesus calls the eye
"the lamp of the body."
And the eye has three circles
(sclera, iris, and pupil),
each within the other,
yet is one distinct whole.
This mirrors the Trinity,
who is one single Being,
but also three distinct persons,
Father, Son, and Holy Spirit,
each residing within the other
in an infinitely recursive union.
There are a few conclusions
we can draw from all these facts.

There are also some comparisons
to help define male and female
by examining the sun and moon,
and comparing them to Adam and Eve,
as well as to Jesus and Mary,
to draw some conclusions
about what characteristics
are fitting or not per gender,
and what effects these tend to have.

But there's almost nothing about women's breasts themselves.
There's just a few points made in the whole chapter.

One point is made that they are entirely meant for others,
and how that connects to the psychology and sociology of women,
for example, how this fact causes resentment within some women.

Another point makes connections between men's and women's breasts,
again for the sake of discovering through inference ideal gender roles,
drawing out philosophical principles from literal and physical facts.

Obviously I can't go into more detail here,
as then I'd just be repeating what's in my book.

Anyway, I hope this clears things up,
and explains what I meant.
