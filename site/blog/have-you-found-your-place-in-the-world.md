### Inspiration

I'm convinced I'll never find my place in this world.
That's why I chose this topic today.

### Discussions

I admit, I don't remember most of the conversations.

Some people said wherever they are is their place in the world.

Some people named specific places.
One person said her home country is her place.
Another person said he moved here and America is his place.

One young black man said music is his place in the world.

A couple people said when they find love or have a family
that will be their place in the world.

Some people said there is no place for any of us,
we just have to make do with each moment.

One young man from Moody Bible Institute
stopped and talked with me for a while.
I don't remember our conversation,
except that he said he liked my book topics.

One young autistic woman (she said she was diagnosed)
stopped and talked with me for a while.
I was enjoying our conversation
and told her I appreciated how thoughtful she was,
and said I related to a lot of the ways she thinks.
I especially respected the fact that she lives on principle,
for example that she said she doesn't shave her legs.
But I tried to pull her out of people's way
and she said that made her uncomfortable.
I don't understand how to socialize, at all.

Anyway, I got her number, and then told her my intentions,
which was to see what it's like to fall in love,
but with radical celibacy, that is, no sexuality at all.
I told her this after telling her the same thing backfired
with a young redheaded woman that I met last week.

### My answer

I don't think there's any place for me in this world.
I've tried for so long to make friends here, to meet people here.
It always fails or backfires or ends abruptly.

I can't find my people.
And I'm too old and tired to keep looking.

Maybe that's tomorrow's survey question:
"Have you found your people?"
