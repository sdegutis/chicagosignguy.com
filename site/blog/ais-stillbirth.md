If you're Catholic, this should be *very* interesting.
If you're not, this should be *somewhat* interesting.

According to standard Catholic theology,
interpreting the Bible must be done
very similar to the game Sudoku.
Each logical conclusion must be drawn
very patiently, carefully, and correctly.
But once established, it becomes a block
which we can build more blocks on top of[^1].

And the Book of Revelation is a classic example
of a book that we know *very* little about.
Therefore, the most prudent thing to do usually,
is to just *not* interpret that book,
and take with a heavy dose of salt
any comprehensive interpretations anyone gives.

That said, the Vatican has *officially* weighed in
on at least one verse, Revelation 13:15, recently.
In the document *[Antiqua et nova]* published in 2025,
the Vatican *directly* connects this verse to AI.

For context, the document was published by the DDF,
or the Dicastery for the Doctrine of the Faith,
meaning it is essentially of *the most official level*
that *any* Catholic document can be.
Popes can make off the cuff remarks,
or make statements on airplanes,
or give opinions in homilies,
and none of these *need* be believed.
But a document from the DDF
essentially *is to be believed* by Catholics.

Okay, so hopefully this establishes the fact
that the Vatican connected a verse in Revelation to AI.
But what does it mean, exactly?
What inferences or conclusions can we draw?

First, the quote from [Antiqua et nova] (¶ 105):

> Moreover, AI may prove even more seductive than traditional idols for,
> unlike idols that “have mouths but do not speak; eyes, but do not see; ears, but do not hear” (Ps. 115:5-6),
> AI can “speak,” or at least gives the illusion of doing so (cf. Rev. 13:15).

And the Biblical verse (RSVCE translation) itself simply says:

> and it was allowed to give breath
> to the image of the beast
> so that the image of the beast
> should even speak,
> and to cause those
> who would not worship
> the image of the beast
> to be slain.

I *highly* recommend reading the contexts
of both the Biblical passage,
and the Vatican's document,
because they're just extremely interesting.
